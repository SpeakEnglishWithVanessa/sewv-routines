# SEWV Support Knowledge Base — 01: Email Response Playbook

For each email type: how to recognize it, how to respond, and what admin follow-up (if any) the human needs to do after. Admin steps reference full procedures in 02-admin-task-procedures.md.

**Updated October 29, 2026:** FFC members now study in the learning platform (https://club.speakenglishwithvanessa.com). Speaking classes and live calls are always answered with "go to the Live page." Don't send the Teachable sign-in link unless file 00 "READ FIRST" #2 allows it. For current members writing about the learning platform or speaking classes, the FFC Learning Platform Support Guide comes first.

## Interpreting ESL student language

Any mention of "subscription," "membership," a recurring/monthly payment, or "charged again" — however vaguely worded — should be read as referring to the Fearless Fluency Club Monthly Membership ($35/month, first month $5 via the standard /fluency checkout page). Don't assume a different product (Yearly, Lifetime, a Challenge, Vanessa AI) unless specified otherwise.

"The app," "the new platform," "club.speakenglishwithvanessa.com," "the code," "Vanessa AI chat," "Solo," "gems" or "the Live page" from a member almost always means the new FFC learning platform — use the FFC Learning Platform Support Guide.

## Recalibration: default to delete unless there's a real reason to reply

Self-assessment, confirmed by Vanessa: the root-cause failure mode across many corrected threads was using "does a KB template match this message" as the signal for "does this deserve a reply," rather than "is this a serious, actionable inquiry from someone likely to be or become a paying customer, or an existing customer with an actual account matter." The knowledge base has a template for nearly everything (hardship, "are you real," bare interest, personal chit-chat) because it covers the full range of things people say — not because every instance deserves a reply given daily volume.

**Default to Claude/Need deleted unless a message is:** (a) from an existing paying customer with a genuine account/billing/access issue, or (b) contains a specific, concrete, on-topic question that signals real interest or purchase intent.

This explicitly includes routing to delete:

- Personal/conversational engagement bait ("How are you? How did you get my email?", "are you real?") — don't engage even though a KB template technically covers the surface pattern.
- Bare "do you have anything free" asks with no personalization or investment in the message.
- Bare, no-detail inquiries — a message with essentially no content beyond "I need to learn English" or similar, with no specifics about goals, level, timeline, or an actual question. Contrast with genuinely low-effort-but-real questions ("is this free or paid," "which classes, beginner or advanced") — those still get a reply. The distinguishing factor is whether there's any specific question or detail, not message length.
- **Refining "bare, no-detail" for short replies to broadcasts:** the test isn't message length, it's whether the message names something concrete. A short reply that names a specific product feature, format, or skill ("I need a speaking partner to help me practice," "I want to improve my listening") has enough to reply to — Claude/Need sent, generic FFC sales-page info matched to whatever was named (not a full multi-bullet template, just the relevant piece). A short reply that only restates the general goal every prospect already has ("help me learn English," "please help me teacher," "I want to speak fluently" with nothing more specific) stays a delete. The test: is there a concrete peg to hang a reply on (a named feature/format/skill), or is it just "help me" in different words?
- Vague "I need help" with no indication of the actual problem — don't draft a reply asking them to clarify; just delete. Don't spend a reply fishing for information the student didn't volunteer. This is especially common as a reply to a free-worksheet/lead-magnet email; replies to free-resource emails are lower-signal by default than replies to actual course/account emails.
- Vague one-line replies to a general newsletter/marketing broadcast with no context (e.g. "this is not working," no detail on what "this" is) — even though it superficially reads like a complaint/question. Contrast with a short reply to a course-related email as a paying student (learning-platform emails, live-class reminder, worksheet confirmation, receipt) — that's worth replying to even if short, since there's real account context.

**Exception that overrides this rule: replies to the learning-platform launch/welcome emails and the 7-day feedback challenge are never Claude/Need deleted, even with no concrete ask** (see the FFC Learning Platform Support Guide). Receiving those emails already confirms membership, and the point is to invite personal sharing. A message with pure enthusiasm and no specific question still gets a warm acknowledgment reply. (The old Day 4/Day 6 emails are retired — see #27.)

When still uncertain: would a template-matching reply actually move this person toward being a customer, or would it just be a polite response to noise? If the honest answer is "probably noise," delete it. When genuinely unsure and none of the above resolves it, default toward Claude/Need deleted — Vanessa reviews that folder daily, so a mistaken deletion is a lower-cost error than an unnecessary or wrong Need sent/Need admin draft. If it's genuinely important but doesn't fit any bucket cleanly, use the VANESSA label instead of leaving it unlabeled — see file 04's labeling rule.

### 1. Recurring payment declined / "system won't accept my card"

**Recognize:** Monthly Membership student, payment failed, asks to pay manually. **Respond:** Usually the bank/card blocking a recurring charge, not a system error — they need to tell their card company to accept the recurring $35 charge. No manual/one-off payment option exists for Monthly Membership. **Admin follow-up:** None immediately.

### 2. Cancel Monthly Membership request

**Recognize:** "Please cancel my subscription." **Respond:** Confirm canceled — no further payments, welcome to rejoin anytime. (Their access to the learning platform ends with the membership.) Whenever a reply discusses cancellation as an *option* (not an active request), explicitly offer to do it for them rather than just describing that it's possible: "If you'd like me to cancel your membership for you, just let me know!" **Admin follow-up:** Procedure A.

### 3. "Why did you cancel?" follow-up (auto cancellation survey)

**Recognize:** Subject often "I have a quick question." **Respond:** Validate their stated reason, remind them they can still access anything already purchased, welcome back anytime. De-escalate politely if hostile. **Admin follow-up:** None.

### 4. Rejoin Monthly Membership after a break

**Recognize:** Past student wants back in. **Respond:** Welcome back, give the full-price checkout link (no coupon — they're not a new student). Tell them to buy with the **same email address they used before** (their original account), not a new one — accounts can't be merged, and the learning platform recognizes them by that email. After joining, they sign in at https://club.speakenglishwithvanessa.com with that email. **Admin follow-up:** None special; watch for tag issues (see #5).

### 5. Reused the $5 new-student coupon multiple times

**Recognize:** Same person cycling join→cancel→join with the intro coupon. **Respond:** Note kindly that the coupon is for new students only; going forward it'll be $35/month. **Admin follow-up:** Check Kit tags are correct.

### 6. Failed/unsuccessful payment ("transaction declined," "my card isn't working")

**Recognize:** A **specific payment method that's failing or being declined** — distinct from #19 (no payment method exists at all, which routes to delete). If wording is ambiguous: "I can't complete my purchase" / "my card isn't working" / "transaction declined" = this entry; a bare "I can't pay" / "I have no money" with no mention of an attempted transaction = #19.

**The test:** does the message describe an attempted-and-failed transaction (→ this entry, reply), or an absence of any way to pay at all (→ #19, delete)? Statements like "I have no mastercard," "I don't have any online or card payment," and "no bank account" are all the no-payment-method case (→ delete), regardless of phrasing — even though they mention "card" or "pay," which can mis-trigger this entry if read too quickly.

**Respond (for genuine #6 cases):** Usually (a) a card that doesn't allow international purchases, or (b) doesn't allow recurring charges. Checklist: must be a real credit card (Visa/Mastercard, not debit, not a gift card) approved for international purchases. Debit and PayPal don't work for *subscription* payments but do work for one-time-payment courses. Bank transfers, checks, crypto not accepted. **Known limited-card-access countries** (fine to explain directly without digging further): India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Russia, Kenya. **Admin follow-up:** Check Stripe for the decline reason and Teachable for a duplicate account — except for the listed countries, where this step can be skipped.

### 7. "I paid, but the site asks me to pay again" / can't access purchased course

**Recognize:** Login confusion, not a real double-charge. **Respond:** 95% of the time they're just not signed in, or they're on the wrong site. For FFC members: send them to https://club.speakenglishwithvanessa.com and ask them to sign in with the email they used to join (they'll get a 6-digit code by email; no password). For a non-FFC product (a challenge), they sign in on the course website. Confirm Kit tags/Teachable purchases first. **Admin follow-up:** None if it's just login.

### 7b. "I couldn't connect" / "can't connect" — access/login trouble

**Recognize:** Bare or vague messages using the word "connect" ("I couldn't connect," "can't connect," "sorry I couldn't connect") — even with zero other detail. This names a specific kind of problem (access) and should **not** be treated as a content-free "help me" message. It can mean can't sign in to the learning platform, can't join a speaking class, can't get into the Facebook group, or can't reach Vanessa AI — the student often won't specify which.

**Respond:** Route to **Claude/Need admin**. Look the student up to confirm active membership before finalizing the reply. Once confirmed, the reply should include at minimum the learning platform link (https://club.speakenglishwithvanessa.com) with "sign in with the email you used to join," plus "for speaking classes, go to the Live page and tap the class." Only add the Teachable sign-in link if the thread shows they mean a non-FFC product or the old course website. For anything about the learning platform itself, follow the FFC Learning Platform Support Guide's sign-in section.

**Admin follow-up:** Account lookup to confirm membership status before finalizing.

### 8. No confirmation email / misspelled email at signup

**Recognize:** "I paid but never got anything," receipt shows a different email. **Respond:** Explain you're looking into it; follow Procedure D (business hours) or E (after hours). Note: the learning platform only recognizes the email on their purchase, so a misspelled purchase email also blocks learning-platform sign-in until it's corrected. **Admin follow-up:** Procedure D or E, plus F if there's a duplicate bounced Kit entry.

### 9. Bounced email in Kit

**Recognize:** Especially iCloud/Yahoo/Hotmail. **Respond:** Explain the provider is blocking automated messages, not their fault. **Admin follow-up:** Procedure G.

### 10. Can't download PDF / worksheet link doesn't work

**Recognize:** Especially Naver, or blocked providers. **Respond:** Explain some providers strip download buttons; have them safelist the sender. Send the PDF directly as a last resort. **Admin follow-up:** None.

### 11. Refund request — general

**Recognize:** Any "can I get a refund." **Respond:** Default is yes, no questions asked, even outside 30 days — avoids costly disputes. Be warm and brief.

- Subscription products (Monthly, Yearly): confirm refunded/canceled, and that they're welcome to rejoin anytime. Don't mention access timing either way.
- One-time products (Challenges, Lifetime): keep lifetime access even after refund.
- Escalate to Vanessa/Ailsa first if: purchase is 6–12+ months old, heavy usage, no clear reason — possible abuse pattern. **Admin follow-up:** Procedure C/J/K as applicable, plus the cancel tag in Kit.

### 12. Stripe dispute/chargeback notice

**Recognize:** Internal notification, no customer ever emails about this directly. **Respond:** No customer email. **Always route to Claude/Need deleted, full stop — no verification step, no admin follow-up.** We no longer contest disputes (evidence submission rarely wins). This applies even for the seemingly-reasonable instinct to first confirm in Teachable that the membership was actually canceled — skip that check entirely and delete on sight.

### 13. Grant early/full access to a challenge

**Recognize:** "Can I unlock everything at once." **Respond:** Fine for any course once past the start date. Evergreen/self-paced (Listening Challenge packs) has no restriction at all. Live/date-bound courses just need the official start date to have passed. **Admin follow-up:** Procedure L.

### 14. Can't find this month's module / where are the lessons

**Recognize:** Active member confused about the current module or where to study. **Respond:** Send them to the learning platform (https://club.speakenglishwithvanessa.com). Tapping **Learn** opens this month's lessons, and the yellow **"Click here to study the next lesson"** button takes them straight to their next lesson. Other months are in the **Fearless Fluency Library** (earlier months are under "Past lessons"). For older modules, they tap **"Click here to view all lessons"** inside the learning platform. Don't send the 2026 Study Plan PDF. **Admin follow-up:** None.

### 15. Personalized/one-off discount request

**Recognize:** Long-time student asking for a special deal. **Respond:** One standing policy needing no approval: a student (past/present) with $200+ total historical FFC spend qualifies for $650 off Lifetime ($350 total); under $200, $550 off ($450 total, the standard Lifetime promo price). Use total historical spend, not time-in-membership. Anything else: confirm with Vanessa/Ailsa first. **Admin follow-up:** Procedure N.

### 15b. Discount / "too expensive" requests on payment-plan-eligible products

**Recognize:** A prospect asks for a discount, or says the price is too high, for one of these specific products, each of which has a standing payment plan: **150 Day Listening Challenge Bundle** (4×$60/mo), **FFC Lifetime** (4×$125/mo), **90 Day English Challenge** (3×$47), **60 Day Word-Boosting Challenge** (3×$35).

**Respond:** This is not automatically a #19 hardship-delete. Reply with the relevant payment plan. **The wording test:** "can I get a discount," "this is too expensive," "I can't pay it all at once" → reply with the payment plan (this counts as real purchase interest, not hardship). "I have no money," "I don't have a card," "I have no bank account" → still #19, delete, regardless of which product they named.

**Admin follow-up:** None unless they follow up wanting to actually enroll on the plan.

### 16. "What course should I take?"

**Recognize:** Vague or specific guidance requests. **Respond:** Default to Monthly Membership only — $5 first month, then $35/month, sales page https://speakenglishwithvanessa.com/fluency/. Don't mention Yearly/Lifetime/Challenges/Vanessa AI unless specifically asked. If they ask specifically about listening or the 30 Day Listening Challenge, it **is** still sold: $99, https://speakenglishwithvanessa.com/listening/. **PayPal exception:** if the student specifically wants PayPal, recommend the 30 Day Listening Challenge: Passion instead (PayPal doesn't work for the recurring Monthly Membership). Checkout link: http://speak-english-with-vanessa.teachable.com/purchase?product_id=4619323&couponcode=39OFF **Admin follow-up:** None.

### 17. Persistent/pushy demands for 1:1 attention

**Recognize:** Repeated urgent-toned emails, requests to talk live/video/WhatsApp, escalating demands, disrespectful tone about response time. Distinct from someone anxiously following up on a legitimate real request (e.g. a refund) — that's not "pushy," handle the underlying request normally. This entry is specifically unprofessional/entitled persistence without a legitimate request being ignored. **Respond:** Zero tolerance, no reply. Hold the boundary — no 1:1 lessons; all live interaction is the group speaking classes on the Live page in the learning platform. Don't reward escalating urgency with a faster response. **Admin follow-up:** Unsubscribe (Procedure O); label Claude/Possible spam or Claude/Need deleted.

### 18. Collaboration/sponsorship/brand-deal pitches

**Recognize:** Partnership, guest feature, ad placement, product review pitches, including follow-up nudges. **Respond:** No reply at all, not even a decline — responding invites repeated follow-up. **Exception:** if genuinely, clearly language-related (not just tangentially mentioning English/education), flag for Vanessa to look at — everything else gets deleted unseen. **Admin follow-up:** Label Claude/Need deleted (or flag for Vanessa for the language-related exception).

### 19. Financial hardship / can't afford / can't pay — ALWAYS delete, no exceptions

**Recognize:** Any message mentioning lack of money, can't afford it, government restrictions, sanctions, or internet/connectivity issues preventing access or payment — including politically or economically sensitive contexts (embargoed countries, war, refugee status, currency restrictions). Also covers "I don't have a bank account" (same underlying case, different wording).

**Respond: Claude/Need deleted, no reply, regardless of length, tone, or how heartfelt it reads.** There is no "long, heartfelt message gets a warm reply" exception — that distinction has been retracted; treat short one-liners and long heartfelt explanations identically. Volume is high, so this needs to be a fast, decisive read, not a case-by-case judgment call. When in doubt about whether a message counts, err toward deleting — if a message is even partly about money, affordability, payment access, sanctions, government blocking, or connectivity, that's the signal.

**Distinguishing from #6:** a valid card that's failing/declining is #6 (reply, troubleshoot). No viable payment method at all — including "I have no mastercard," "I don't have any online or card payment" — is this entry (delete), not #6, regardless of the specific wording used.

**Distinguishing from 15b:** a discount/"too expensive" ask on a payment-plan-eligible product (150 Day Listening Challenge, FFC Lifetime, 90 Day Challenge, WBC) isn't automatically this entry — check 15b before defaulting to delete. The line: "too expensive / can I get a discount" on one of those products → 15b, reply with the payment plan. "I have no money at all / no card / no bank account" → this entry, delete, on any product.

**Distinguishing from a refund request:** this entry is about students who never paid and are explaining why. A student who already paid and wants it back ("please refund my charge") is a completely different category — still gets a reply + admin follow-up (see #11). The line: already paid and wants it back → reply + admin action. Never paid / can't pay and explaining why → delete, no reply. **Admin follow-up:** None (no reply is sent, so no promo-tag follow-up applies either — that exception has also been retracted along with the "heartfelt" reply exception).

### 20. Angry / hostile / rude emails

**Recognize:** Insults, aggressive tone. **Respond:** No reply needed. **Admin follow-up:** Unsubscribe (Procedure O).

### 20b. Plain-language unsubscribe requests ("stop your gmail," "stop emailing me," "stop sending me emails")

**Recognize:** Any request to stop receiving emails, worded in plain/simple language rather than the word "unsubscribe" itself — most ESL students won't use that word. Applies whether the tone is polite or rude.

**Respond: No reply is ever sent for these.** Don't confirm the unsubscribe, don't explain the self-service unsubscribe link, don't send anything customer-facing at all — just unsubscribe them in Kit and apply the **Claude/Possible unsubscribe** label. This is not a Claude/Need admin (high priority) case — that label is reserved for things needing real account lookups (refunds, enrollment checks, coupon creation, etc.), not a single simple unsubscribe action.

**Admin follow-up:** Procedure O (unsubscribe).

### 21. Emails to delete without response

**Recognize:** Pure "thank you," "OK teacher," single-word replies, empty content, requests outside scope (visa sponsorship, personal favors), duplicate/self-sent newsletter copies (sender = recipient = contact@speakenglishwithvanessa.com), spam-folder items with no real inquiry. Also: personal contact requests (WhatsApp, Instagram, phone number, visa/immigration help, or generally wanting to chat personally outside of email) — route straight to delete, no boundary-redirect reply needed. **Respond:** No reply, just delete (unsubscribe first if they indicated they don't want the sales emails they're on). **Admin follow-up:** Occasionally unsubscribe (Procedure O).

**Note:** plain-language unsubscribe requests are now their own entry — see 20b — rather than being handled as a delete-with-optional-unsubscribe case.

### 22. Course check-in response (student replies to automated "how's it going")

**Recognize:** Subject often "Quick question" (same automation family as the cancel-survey, different trigger). **Respond:** Warm acknowledgment of whatever they shared; offer further support if they're a paying student with a real ask. **Admin follow-up:** None.

### 23. Confusion about differing content between students

**Recognize:** Comparing notes, confused about differing modules. **Respond:** All three formats (Monthly, Yearly, Lifetime) grant access to the same full module library — the old one-module-per-month system was discontinued, so differences are more likely one student not having found the right place yet. Send them to the learning platform (see #14): **Learn** for this month's lessons, the **Fearless Fluency Library** for other months, and "Click here to view all lessons" for older modules. The Intermediate and Advanced courses are **not** part of the membership and are no longer sold (see file 03). **Admin follow-up:** None.

### 24. "I unsubscribed but was still charged"

**Recognize:** Confused newsletter-unsubscribe with paid-membership-cancellation — these are different actions. **Respond:** Clarify kindly (common, not their fault), confirm canceling now and refunding the erroneous charge. **Admin follow-up:** Procedure A + refund the specific charge.

### 25. General formatting reminders

### 26. China-specific situations

### 28. Teacher AI (Peter) forwarded/CC'd support ticket — course vs. app clarification

**Recognize:** Automated Zendesk email from support@teacherai.zendesk.com, subject line "[Teacher AI] Re: Support request from [NAME]," CC'ing contact@speakenglishwithvanessa.com. Peter (Vanessa AI app support, see file 03/04) is asking the student to clarify whether their question is about the Vanessa AI app or one of Vanessa's courses, and looped Vanessa's team in specifically in case it's course-related. **Not** an internal-only notification like a Stripe dispute (#12) — a real student is CC'd and may be waiting on an answer, so don't default-delete it the way an automated notice would be.

Note: the Vanessa AI practice **inside the FFC learning platform** is part of the Fearless Fluency Club, not Peter's separate app. Questions about it follow the FFC Learning Platform Support Guide, not Peter.

**Respond:** Read the student's original message, quoted further down in the Zendesk thread, to determine which product they're actually asking about — don't wait on the student's clarification if the quoted content already makes it clear (course-specific language like "when does my course start," a specific challenge/module name, etc. usually settles it). Reply directly to the student (CC isn't required on the reply). **Route to Claude/Need admin, not Claude/Need deleted** — this needs an account lookup to confirm which product (course purchase vs. Vanessa AI subscription) the student actually has before the reply can be finalized, since the Zendesk thread alone doesn't confirm it. Once confirmed, respond using the relevant product's normal playbook guidance (course content questions per files 01/03; Vanessa AI issues beyond cancellation and login checks get Peter cc'd on the reply per file 03, not sent back to the student to contact separately).

**Admin follow-up:** Check Kit/Teachable for the student's actual enrollment/purchase to confirm which product the question belongs to.

### 29. Newsletter subscribers / non-students asking for open-ended English help

**Recognize:** A newsletter subscriber or general prospect asks something like "Can I ask you English questions here?" or "Can you help me with my English?" — a request for ongoing, open-ended English support over email, distinct from a specific course question or a specific lesson-content question from an enrolled student.

**Respond:** We do not offer general, unlimited English-question support over email, for non-students or enrolled students. Email scope is limited to: (a) questions about joining a course, for non-students/prospects, and (b) questions about lesson content inside a course, for enrolled students. Check Kit for a membership tag (per file 04's membership-verification step) to know which version applies. For non-students, warmly explain the scope and redirect to the Fearless Fluency Club sales page. For enrolled students asking for open-ended help, redirect to the learning platform: daily practice with Vanessa AI, and the live speaking classes on the Live page.

**Admin follow-up:** None.
